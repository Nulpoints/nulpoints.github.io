ASLwrite Keyboard

ASLwrite is an open-source, iconic featural writing system and orthography developed specifically for American Sign Language (ASL). Originally created in 2011 by Adrean Clark and Julia Dameron, it evolved from si5s (which itself derived from SignWriting) into a public-domain system designed to capture the rich phonemic and visual nuances of sign language. Maintained as a community-driven orthography, ASLwrite provides a practical way to transcribe ASL directly in-line from left to right.

The system represents signs through a structured sequence of four primary components: digits (symbols representing handshapes and finger configurations), locatives (characters indicating body or spatial locations from frontal or profile perspectives), diacritics and movement marks (which detail hand orientation, small motions, or directional paths), and non-manual marks (which capture facial expressions like eyebrow movements, mouth shapes, and body shifts). By arranging these elements sequentially, ASLwrite provides a comprehensive script for writing complete signs, phrases, and sentences in American Sign Language.
