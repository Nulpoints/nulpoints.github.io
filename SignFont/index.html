The Story of SignFont

A Landmark 1987 Orthography. Revitalized for the Modern Web.

SignFont was created in 1987 by researcher and linguist Don Newkirk as a linear, keyboard-native writing system for American Sign Language.

The Vision of Don Newkirk (1987)

During the 1980s at the intersection of early computational linguistics and sign language research, researcher Don Newkirk recognized a fundamental void: while spoken languages had thousands of typographic fonts and standard keyboard layouts, American Sign Language users had no native way to type their language on standard computer terminals without cumbersome graphics programs.

"SignFont was engineered to be a true orthography: linear, typable on any standard 101-key keyboard, and phonologically rigorous across 19 basic handshapes and bodily spatial coordinates." — Don Newkirk, The SignFont Handbook (1987)

As proprietary operating systems shifted away from 1980s 8-bit character mapping, the original SignFont font files became unreadable on modern platforms. The SignFont Revival Project is an ongoing work in progress, prototyping custom TrueType font rendering, Keyman keyboard drivers, and modern interactive digital interfaces.

  • 1987 · The Salk Institute Handbook

    Don Newkirk and researchers at the Salk Institute & Emerson and Stern publish the Final Version Signfont Handbook, defining 19 basic handshapes and bodily spatial coordinates.

  • 1990s · The Digital Barrier

    Early desktop operating systems phase out custom character maps, leaving SignFont stored primarily on paper archives.

  • Active · Unicode Adaptation & Discord Coordination

    Unicode character mapping is currently still happening, coordinated and actively discussed on the Signed Conlangs & Linguistics Discord (building upon the Unicode project archive by jan Olipija). Join server →

  • Active · The SignFont Revival (Work in Progress)

    An ongoing, iterative community effort to build accessible web utilities—prototyping font rendering engines, interactive dictionaries, virtual keyboards, converters, and study tools. Nothing is final; the revival is an evolving work in progress.

Linguistic Architecture

The Anatomy of a SignFont Word

Every SignFont sign is constructed from 5 modular phonological tiers, reading smoothly from left to right.

1. Handshape Prime
aQ
Curled Fists (Baby-O)
Base hand configuration from the 19 core handshape classes (a–u) with diacritic modifier.
2. Action Surface
!
Palm Side
Active contact surface: palm side (!), back ("), radial edge (%), or interdigital space (+).
3. Body Location
/ 1
Both Temples
Spatial landmark on head or torso (temples, nose, chin, chest, or neutral space).
4. Motion Vector
H
Circular Path
3D movement trajectory: circular sweep (H), wrist twist (I/J), upward (D), or outward (G).
5. Dynamic Modifier
vy
Dual Hands & Repeated
Both hands identical (v), alternating (w), tense emphasis (z), or repeated (y).
